What this change does: M8 adds a Reportes module with no tables of its own. It calls new read-only report interfaces in each module: sales by day, product, category and cashier with historical-cost profit, shifts, inventory value, DGII 607 and 606 rows, receivables and payables. Each report can be exported as JSON, CSV (UTF-8 with BOM, formula-safe) or Excel. M9 adds a background queue that sends e-CF with backoff, an import and lookup of the DGII RNC registry, an audit chain check (on demand and every night at 03:00), HTTPS with a self-signed node certificate from the Windows store, daily log files, and `reloj`, `cola-ecf` and `auditoria` health checks. It also adds an e2e suite that starts the real API process and runs a whole store day over HTTP.

## Must fix

1. **The 607 puts ITBIS inside "Monto" and mixes card with transfer** (`Ventas/VentasReportes.cs:L79-82`, `Ventas.Contracts/Reportes.cs:L11-13`)
   - **What this is:** `Documentos607Async` makes one row per sale, void credit note and return credit note. The CSV/Excel export is meant to be pasted into the DGII tool (the `shortcut:` in `ReportesModule.cs:L66`).
   - **Problem:** The DGII 607 "Monto Facturado" is the amount *without* taxes, and ITBIS goes in its own column. Toki writes `v.Total` (ITBIS included) as `Monto` and `v.Itbis` next to it. Toki's own 606 does it right (`Monto = Subtotal`, `GastosYPagos.cs:L306`). Example: a sale of 2 refrescos at 59 has 118 with ITBIS 18, so the 607 must say Monto 100, ITBIS 18. Toki says Monto 118, ITBIS 18. For a minimarket where most items pay ITBIS, the declared income is about 18 % too high every month, and it will not match the IT-1 or the e-CF data DGII already has. The payment columns are also merged: `Tarjeta` and `Transferencia` go into one `TarjetaOTransferencia` column, but the 607 has separate columns for "Tarjeta Débito/Crédito" and "Cheque/Transferencia/Depósito". Balance in favor goes to `Otros`.
   - **Fix:** Write `Monto = Total − Itbis` for sales, void credit notes and returns (`x.x.Total − x.x.Itbis`). Split the payment column into `Tarjeta` and `ChequeTransferencia`. Add a test to `ReportesTests.Turnos_inventario_y_607` with sale A (448 with ITBIS 36): its row has `Monto = 412`, `Itbis = 36`, and the payment columns add up to 448. Have the store's accountant confirm two points I am not sure of: what goes in the payment columns of a credit note, and whether e-CF issuers still file 607 rows for documents DGII already received.
   - **If we skip it:** The first owner who files with this export declares more income than they had, and the numbers do not match DGII's own e-CF data.

## Should fix

2. **The sales reports disagree with each other on returns and on what "Neto" means** (`Ventas/VentasReportes.cs:L14-33`, `L35-55`, `L57-65`, `Reportes/ReportesModule.cs:L47-55`)
   - **Problem:** These cases give different numbers for the same days:
     - **Returns land on different days.** `PorDiaAsync` subtracts a return on the day it happened. `PorProductoAsync`, which also feeds `por-categoria`, loads the lines of sales made in the range and subtracts every return ever made on those lines, whatever its date. Example: chuleta sold on 31 October and returned on 2 November. The November report by day subtracts it, but the November reports by product and by category do not. The October report by product subtracts it, so a month that was already closed changes after the fact. Total profit by category for November is then not equal to total profit by day for November.
     - **"Neto" means three things.** In `por-dia` it includes ITBIS (the test expects 566 with ITBIS 36). In `por-producto` and `por-categoria` it excludes ITBIS (530 for the same day). In `por-cajero` it includes ITBIS and does not subtract returns. The owner sees three different "Neto" figures for one day.
   - **Fix:** In `PorProductoAsync`, load returns the way `DevolucionesAsync` does: by the return's `FechaUtc` in the range, joined to their `LineaVenta` for product, factor and cost. A join also replaces the `ids.Contains` over every line of the month. Rename the fields so each name says one thing: `por-dia` has `VentasConItbis` and `NetoSinItbis`, and `por-producto`, `por-categoria` and `por-cajero` use `NetoSinItbis`. For `por-cajero`, either subtract the returns of that cashier's sales or call the column `Vendido`. Test: a sale on day 1 and its return on day 2. For day 2, `por-dia` and the sum of `por-categoria` give the same profit. Day 1's report does not change after the return.
   - **If we skip it:** ADR-0004 promises that reports add up to the closing. They do not even add up to each other, and the owner stops trusting them.

3. **The 606 and 607 rows are missing columns DGII requires** (`Compras.Contracts/Reportes.cs:L6`, `Compras/Features/GastosYPagos.cs:L302-313`, `Ventas.Contracts/Reportes.cs:L11-13`)
   - **Problem:** Even with 1 fixed, the rows cannot be pasted into the DGII tool, because the columns do not line up. The 607 has no "Tipo de Identificación" and no "Tipo de Ingreso" (01 for normal sales). The 606 has no "Forma de Pago" code (01 cash, 02 transfer/check, 03 card, 04 credit and so on). It only has `Contado`/`Credito`, and "contado" can be cash or a transfer. It also has no "Fecha de Pago" and no split between "Monto facturado en bienes" and "en servicios" (electricity is a service, beef is a good). Toki already knows most of this: the payment method of each supplier payment, the expense type, and whether the line is a purchase or an expense. Note that meat is exempt, so the ITBIS paid on purchases for exempt sales may have to go in "ITBIS llevado al costo". That is a question for the accountant.
   - **Fix:** Make `Documento606` and `Documento607` follow the DGII column order. Fill in what Toki knows: `TipoId` for the 607 the same way as for the 606, `TipoIngreso = 1`, bienes for purchases and servicios for expenses of type 2 or 3, and the payment code from the purchase's `Condicion` plus the `Medio` of the payment that paid it. Leave the rest blank. Have the accountant check one exported month before the pilot. A test can check the column names in order.
   - **If we skip it:** Someone retypes every row by hand each month, which is the manual work TK-054 was meant to remove.

4. **An HTTP timeout in the e-CF queue stops the whole server** (`Fiscal/EnvioEcf.cs:L47`, `L78`)
   - **What this is:** Each document's send is wrapped in `catch (Exception ex) when (ex is not OperationCanceledException)`, and the worker loop has the same filter.
   - **Problem:** `HttpClient` reports a timeout as `TaskCanceledException`, which is an `OperationCanceledException`. When the real provider (TK-055) times out, which is the most common network failure, the exception passes both filters and leaves `ExecuteAsync`. In .NET a background service that throws stops the host by default (`BackgroundServiceExceptionBehavior.StopHost`, which this repo does not change). So the Windows service stops, and every register loses the server, because the internet was slow. Today it cannot happen, because the provider is simulated and the documents are `Simulado`. But the class comment says "throws on a transient failure: the document is retried", and the next person will rely on that.
   - **Fix:** Filter on the token, not the exception type: `catch (Exception ex) when (!ct.IsCancellationRequested)` in `ProcesarAsync`, and `when (!stoppingToken.IsCancellationRequested)` in the worker. Add a case to `EnvioEcfTests`: the provider throws `new TaskCanceledException()`, `ProcesarAsync` returns 1, and the document is `Pendiente` with `Intentos = 1`.
   - **If we skip it:** The day the provider is connected, a slow connection shuts the store down.

5. **The nightly audit check never runs on a PC that is off at night, and a restart forgets a broken chain** (`Configuracion/Auditoria.cs:L30-71`)
   - **Problem:** `VerificacionNocturnaAuditoria` first waits until 03:00 and then checks. Many colmados and butcher shops switch the PC off at closing and on again in the morning. The service then starts at 07:00, waits for a 03:00 that never comes while it is running, and is stopped again at night. The check never runs. Also, `UltimaVerificacionAuditoria` keeps its result only in memory. After a restart the `auditoria` health check says Healthy even when last night's check found the chain broken.
   - **Fix:** Check once a few minutes after startup, then every day at 03:00. That is one `Verificar` call before the loop, which is fewer lines than today. Test: start the app with a broken chain and expect `/health` to be Unhealthy without waiting for 03:00 (use the fake clock).
   - **If we skip it:** TK-094 ("detect tampering") only works in stores that leave the PC on all night.

6. **The node certificate needs admin rights on every start** (`Host.Local/CertificadoNodo.cs:L17-18`, `L38-40`, `Program.cs:L23-27`)
   - **Problem:** `ObtenerOCrear` always opens `LocalMachine\My` with `OpenFlags.ReadWrite`, even when it only needs to read the certificate. Only administrators and LocalSystem may do that. The service installer does not exist yet (TK-093 "Falta"), and the architecture asks for keys that "only the service account" can use (`arquitectura-v1-vsa.md` §7.1). If the installer runs the service as a virtual account (`NT SERVICE\Toki`) or as LocalService, the service fails at startup with "access denied", even after an admin created the certificate. If it works around that by running once as an admin, the private key file in `MachineKeys` belongs to that admin, and the service account may not be able to read it. Kestrel then fails on the first TLS handshake.
   - **Fix:** Look the certificate up with `OpenFlags.ReadOnly`, and open the store `ReadWrite` only on the branch that creates one. Let the installer (elevated) create the certificate and give the service account read access to its private key. Write down which account the service uses in the TK-093 notes. A test cannot cover the Windows ACLs. Check it once on a clean Windows VM with the installer.
   - **If we skip it:** The first pilot install either runs the service as LocalSystem, which is more power than it needs, or fails to start.

7. **A year-long report can stall the store server while the registers sell** (`Ventas/VentasReportes.cs:L17-18`, `L41-43`, `Reportes/Exportar.cs:L52-61`, `ReportesModule.cs:L24`)
   - **Problem:** The `shortcut:` on `VentasReportes` says in-memory aggregation is "fine for a store's month". But `Periodo.MaxDias` allows 366 days. A year of `por-dia` loads about 180,000 sales with all 730,000 lines as full entities (with descriptions) into the API process, which is several hundred MB on the PC that also runs SQL Server Express and serves 3 registers. Then the `xlsx` export does ClosedXML `InsertTable` plus `Columns().AdjustToContents()` over every row. ClosedXML keeps every cell as an object, and `AdjustToContents` measures every cell. For a year of 607 rows (about 180,000 rows × 10 columns) that takes a lot of memory and a long run of CPU, while sales wait on the same machine.
   - **Fix:** Two small changes. (a) In `PorDiaAsync`, project one row per sale instead of including the lines: `select new { v.FechaUtc, v.Total, v.Itbis, v.Bruto, v.Descuento, Costo = v.Lineas.Sum(l => l.CostoUnitario * l.Cantidad) }`. A decimal `Sum` in a subquery already works on both engines (`CargosAbiertosAsync` does it). (b) In `Exportar.Excel`, size columns from the first 100 rows only (`AdjustToContents(1, 100)`), and refuse `xlsx` above about 50,000 rows with a message that says to use CSV. Keep the year range for JSON and CSV.
   - **If we skip it:** The owner asks for "ventas del año" in Excel on a Saturday afternoon, and the registers freeze while it runs.

8. **Shift times in CSV and Excel are UTC without saying so** (`Reportes/Exportar.cs:L40`, `Caja.Contracts/Reportes.cs:L4-5`, `CajaModule.cs:L34`)
   - **Problem:** `ResumenTurno.AbiertoUtc`/`CerradoUtc` come out of EF as UTC (`ModuleDbContext` converter). The CSV writes them as `yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm:ss` with no zone, and Excel gets the raw `DateTime`. A shift opened at 08:00 shows "12:00", and one that closed at 22:30 shows the next day at 02:30. JSON is fine, because it carries the `Z`.
   - **Fix:** In `Exportar.Celda` and before `InsertTable`, convert `DateTime` values with `+ DiaNegocio.Desfase` and name the columns without `Utc` in the export (or give the contract `AbiertoLocal`/`CerradoLocal`). Test: one CSV row of `turnos` shows the local opening hour.
   - **If we skip it:** The shift report the owner prints puts late closings on the wrong day.

9. **The riskiest new code has no test that fails when it breaks, and the e2e day does not reconcile the reports** (`tests/Toki.IntegrationTests/Reportes/ReportesTests.cs`, `tests/Toki.E2ETests/DiaDeTiendaTests.cs:L220-222`, `tests/Toki.IntegrationTests/Fiscal/EnvioEcfTests.cs`)
   - **What this is:** The report tests check one sold day well (net, ITBIS, cost, profit, the void, the return, CSV neutralization, Excel). The e2e day is a real step up: a real process, 3 registers in parallel, a restart, fiado, purchases, blind closes, and checks of drawers, kardex, e-NCF gaps, customer and supplier balances and the audit chain. It covers the R4 #14 list, except a return to the customer's account.
   - **Problem:** These behaviors have no test:
     - The 607 values (only the row count is checked), which is how 1 went unnoticed. A return on a later day, which is 2. Voided sales that cross midnight (common in colmados open after 00:00).
     - `EnvioEcf` with a `TaskCanceledException` (4), and a document the provider rejects (`Rechazado` stops retrying).
     - The `auditoria` health check after a broken chain (5). Only `HastaLaProxima` is tested.
     - The e2e day claims "every total must add up", but for reports it only checks the number of sales. It never checks that the reports match the closes and the documents.
     - On SQL Server, 3 parallel registers can deadlock, which the decorator turns into 409 `Ocupado`. `VenderAsync` then fails the whole day on `Ok()`, but a real register would retry with the same `VentaId`. That would make the CI job flaky. I could not run it here.
   - **Fix:** One test per item above. In the e2e, after the closes, add these checks:
     - the sum over the 3 Z reports of (cobros − anulaciones − reembolsos) equals `por-dia` sales with ITBIS
     - the number of 607 rows equals the number of fiscal documents, and the 607 ITBIS equals the documents' ITBIS
     - `inventario/valor` stock equals `/api/inventario/existencias`
     - one return to the customer's account (`SaldoFavor`)
     - `VenderAsync` retries once on 409 `Ocupado` with the same `VentaId`, which also proves that a retried sale is idempotent under load.
   - **If we skip it:** Findings 1, 2 and 4 stay invisible until an accountant or a slow internet line finds them.

## Nice to have

10. **The RNC registry import holds one long transaction and dies with the client's timeout** (`Fiscal/Features/Padron.cs:L43-67`)
    - **Problem:** The DGII file has several hundred thousand rows (the code allows 300 MB). EF inserts them in batches of 42 statements inside one transaction, so on a store PC this likely takes minutes. While it runs, SQL Server escalates to a table lock on `Contribuyentes`, so every RNC lookup at the registers waits for the whole import. The request token is the HTTP request's, so if the back-office `HttpClient` gives up (100 s by default), the import rolls back and can never finish. The file is read as Latin-1. If DGII publishes it as UTF-8 without a BOM, "PÉREZ" is stored as "PÃ‰REZ". The `HashSet` (a few tens of MB) and the batching by 5,000 are fine.
    - **Fix:** Time it once with the real file on SQL Server Express. If it takes more than a minute, run the import in the background with a status endpoint, or use `SqlBulkCopy` on SQL Server into a staging table and swap it in a short transaction. For encoding, try strict UTF-8 first (`new UTF8Encoding(false, true)`) and fall back to Windows-1252 on a decoding error.

11. **The simulated provider marks documents "Aceptado"** (`Fiscal/EnvioEcf.cs:L21-25`)
    - **Problem:** Today only `Pendiente` documents are sent, and the simulated signer makes them `Simulado`, so this never runs. If a real signer arrives before the real provider (or someone forgets to replace the registration) and `Fiscal:EnvioAutomatico` is turned on, real signed e-CF become `Aceptado` with a `SIM-` track id and never reach DGII. The `cola-ecf` health check then shows nothing wrong. Two API processes on one database (a support tech starting the exe next to the service) would also both pick and send the same documents, because the query claims nothing.
    - **Fix:** Have `ProveedorSimulado` return `EstadoEnvio.Simulado`. When TK-055 lands, claim each batch with a conditional `ExecuteUpdate` of `ProximoIntentoUtc` (a lease) before sending.

12. **Health thresholds differ from the architecture, and one rejection keeps the store "Degraded" forever** (`Fiscal/ColaEcfHealthCheck.cs:L13-18`)
    - **Problem:** `arquitectura-v1-vsa.md` §7.3 says Degraded after 24 h and Unhealthy after 60 h. The code says Degraded after 60 h and Unhealthy after 72 h, when the infraction has already happened. Any `Rechazado` document ever makes it Degraded, and nothing ever clears it, so after the first rejection the signal means nothing. `/health` itself only returns the overall status word, so there is no information leak, and each call costs at most 3 indexed queries.
    - **Fix:** Use the documented thresholds, and count only rejections without a credit note (or from the last 30 days).

13. **The e2e harness can leave the API running, and the day can split at midnight** (`tests/Toki.E2ETests/ServidorToki.cs:L77-104`, `L130-135`, `DiaDeTiendaTests.cs:L33`, `L221`)
    - **Problem:** If the API fails to answer `/health` within 90 s, `ArrancarAsync` throws, but the process keeps running. `IniciarAsync` never returns a server, so `await using` disposes nothing. On a developer PC that orphan keeps its port and its temp SQLite file. `PuertoLibre` releases the port before the API binds it, so another process can take it in between (rare). `hoy` is taken once. A run that starts at 23:59:50 Santo Domingo time puts some sales on the next business day, and `.Single()` on `por-dia` fails.
    - **Fix:** In `ArrancarAsync`, kill the process before throwing. For the reports, query `desde=hoy&hasta=hoy+1`, or set the API's clock if a fake clock is ever exposed.

14. **Inventory value leaves out inactive products that still have stock** (`Inventario/InventarioReportes.cs:L16`)
    - **Problem:** If the owner deactivates "Queso de hoja" with 20 units left, those units disappear from the stock value, and the value no longer matches the kardex.
    - **Fix:** `where p.Activo || existencias has stock` (filter after loading the stock dictionary). Add an `Activo` column.

15. **Deleting the newest audit records and rewinding the head is not detected** (`BuildingBlocks/Persistence/OutboxYAuditoria.cs:L85-96`)
    - **Problem:** The new tail check correctly catches deleted newest rows when the head row is left alone (tested). Someone with database access can also set `AuditoriaCabeza` back to the last row they keep, and then the chain verifies as intact. Nothing outside the database remembers how far the chain went.
    - **Fix:** Print the head's `Secuencia` and the first 8 hex characters of its hash on each Z report. The paper trail then shows a rewind. That is one line in the Z model and one in the print layout.
